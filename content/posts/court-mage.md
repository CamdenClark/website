I've been working with a lot of non-technical groups trying to implement AI recently—think property managers, wealth management firms, and similar organizations.

There are tons of opportunities to automate manual processes with AI and build internal tools. But largely, these organizations don't even know where to start. The number of tools is huge, and there's very little time for people in their current roles to explore or try things out, much less maintain them.

My prediction is that there will be a new role: **Court Mage**. Or, in more boring terms, an “AI Ops” person.

If we look at the introduction of personal computing to organizations, the IT role emerged to build, deploy, and maintain these solutions for the team. Personal computers offered an obvious benefit to teams, but it was hard to quantify their ROI or manage them without an expert. Implementation required deep expertise in the business's processes, plus a sense of what was possible. If you relied on vendors or consultants, you had a much more difficult alignment problem.

Similarly, there will be a new role here for AI specialists. Most AI “solutions” don't have a clear ROI, and you wouldn't want to spend time listing them all out anyway. You need someone who understands the technology and can provide guidance from the perspective of the organization.

Here's an example. At a property management firm, someone has to download receipts for all of their properties from the city utilities portal—which, of course, has no API—and upload them to the property management SaaS tool. So someone in the organization built a tool that uses ChatGPT's browser navigation to do this.

But this solution doesn't work for all of the properties. And what happens if the site changes and it needs updates? Is it really that person's role to go fix this automation?

Organizations could hire someone to own the tools they build, train staff to build their own, and save everyone a ton of time.

Hire someone to do this for your organization. Your team will be able to spend more time doing the things that drive your business instead of doing data entry or clicking buttons.
